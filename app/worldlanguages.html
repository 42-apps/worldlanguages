World Languages Explorer

A living map of the world's languages. Each country is coloured by its primary (native) language; hover for the full mix of mother tongues. Drag the slider — or hit play — to watch languages rise, spread and morph from antiquity to today: Latin giving way to French and Spanish, Arabic sweeping across the Near East, English carried around the world.

Please read: figures are approximate and illustrative — shares of the resident population by native/primary language. Modern figures draw on national censuses and Ethnologue; deep-history figures are best-effort scholarly estimates, and the deeper the past, the more uncertain. The map uses modern borders at all eras as a visualization device — ancient peoples, not today's nations, occupied that land. Languages are grouped by family (a shared hue), and classical or extinct tongues (Latin, Greek, Sumerian, Aramaic, Gaulish…) appear in their eras.

Sources: national censuses · Ethnologue · UNESCO · Glottolog & WALS · historical-linguistics scholarship. Borders: Natural Earth. Globe: globe.gl.

🗺️ The flat map

Every country is filled proportionally by language — so you see each nation's whole mix of mother tongues at a glance, not just the majority.

📊
Proportional fill. Each country fills from the bottom up by share. A nation that's 60% French & 40% Dutch shows the bottom 60% red, the top 40% blue — the taller a colour, the bigger that language.
